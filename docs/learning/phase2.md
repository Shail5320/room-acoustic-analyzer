# Phase 2: DSP Core & Analysis Pipeline (Pure JavaScript)

## 1. Concepts in Plain Language

In Phase 0, we established a mathematical ground truth using Python, NumPy, and SciPy. In Phase 2, we reimplemented the entire digital signal processing (DSP) pipeline in **pure JavaScript (ES modules)** without external numerical libraries. The code runs both inside Node.js for automated testing and directly inside client-side Web Workers in the browser.

### 1.1 The Cooley–Tukey Radix-2 Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)
The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) converts a time-domain sequence of $N$ audio samples into its frequency spectrum. A direct matrix computation requires $N^2$ complex multiplications. For a 5-second audio capture at $48\text{ kHz}$ zero-padded to $N = 2^{19} = 524{,}288$ samples, $N^2 \approx 2.75 \times 10^{11}$ operations, which would freeze a mobile phone for minutes.

The Cooley–Tukey Radix-2 algorithm exploits divide-and-conquer symmetry: it decomposes a length-$N$ DFT into two length-$(N/2)$ DFTs (even and odd indexed samples), reducing time complexity to:
$$\mathcal{O}(N \log_2 N)$$
At $N = 524{,}288$, $N \log_2 N \approx 10^7$ operations—executable in less than $80\text{ ms}$ on modern mobile hardware.

### 1.2 The Farina Exponential Sine Sweep
To measure room acoustics without firing starter pistols or popping balloons, we use an exponential sine sweep (Angelo Farina, 2000). The frequency increases exponentially with time from $f_1 = 50\text{ Hz}$ to $f_2 = 8000\text{ Hz}$ over $T = 5\text{ s}$.
Because the sweep spends exponentially more time at lower frequencies than at higher frequencies, its energy distribution drops at $-3\text{ dB/octave}$ (pink noise characteristic).

### 1.3 Frequency-Domain Deconvolution & Harmonic Separation
When the room's microphone capture $y(t)$ is convolved with the **inverse filter** $x_\text{inv}(t)$ (the time-reversed sweep tapered by an amplitude envelope of $+6\text{ dB/octave}$), the linear impulse response compresses into an intense spike ($> 40\text{ dB}$ peak-to-sidelobe ratio).
Crucially, any non-linear harmonic distortion generated by cheap phone speakers (2nd, 3rd, 4th harmonics) travels faster than the fundamental chirp. Upon deconvolution, all harmonic distortion products appear **before** the direct sound impulse, leaving the linear Room Impulse Response (RIR) completely uncorrupted.

### 1.4 Butterworth Octave Band Filtering & Filter Ringing
Human pitch perception is logarithmic: octave bands double in frequency at each stage:
$$125\text{ Hz}, \quad 250\text{ Hz}, \quad 500\text{ Hz}, \quad 1000\text{ Hz}, \quad 2000\text{ Hz}, \quad 4000\text{ Hz}$$
Each band is isolated using a 4th-order Butterworth bandpass filter ($N=2$ design order, implemented as 2 Second-Order Sections / biquads).
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why $N=2$ (4th-order total) and not $N=4$ (8th-order)?**  
> Filters with steep stopband roll-off have high $Q$-factor poles that store energy and "ring" in the time domain. At $125\text{ Hz}$, an 8th-order filter's natural impulse response decay lasts $100\text{–}200\text{ ms}$. If the filter rings longer than the room itself, you measure the filter's decay rather than the room! $N=2$ provides standard $12\text{ dB/octave}$ attenuation while keeping filter ringing short enough for accurate acoustic decay measurement.

### 1.5 Schroeder Backward Integration
Squaring an impulse response $h^2(t)$ gives the instantaneous power decay. However, diffuse room reflections arrive with pseudo-random phases, producing a jagged decay curve where fitting a straight line is statistically unstable.
Manfred Schroeder (1965) proved that the ensemble average over infinite random decays equals the **backward cumulative energy integral** of a single measured decay:
$$EDC(t) = \int_t^\infty h^2(\tau) \, d\tau$$
Because energy is summed backward from the quiet tail to the energetic direct arrival, the resulting Energy Decay Curve (EDC) is monotonic, smooth, and deterministic.

### 1.6 The Noise-Floor Problem & Simplified Truncation
In real measurements, the tail of $h(t)$ contains background acoustic noise ($w(t)$ with variance $\sigma^2 > 0$). If integration continues through the noise floor to the end of the buffer:
$$\int_t^T (h_\text{clean}^2(\tau) + \sigma^2) \, d\tau = \int_t^T h_\text{clean}^2(\tau) \, d\tau + \sigma^2(T - t)$$
The linear ramp $\sigma^2(T - t)$ flattens the decay curve on a logarithmic (dB) scale into an artificial horizontal plateau. Fitting a line across this plateau inflates measured RT60 from $0.8\text{ s}$ to over $9\text{ s}$!
The **simplified noise-floor truncation** algorithm detects where the signal drops to $3\text{ dB}$ ($2\times \sigma^2$) above the noise floor, truncates backward integration at that point, and subtracts steady noise power with zero clamping.

### 1.7 Sabine & Eyring Room Acoustics: Dimensionless vs Physical Treatment
- **Sabine's Formula:** $RT60 = \frac{0.161 \cdot V}{A}$, where $V$ is room volume in $\text{m}^3$ and $A = \sum S_i \alpha_i$ is total absorption in metric Sabins ($\text{m}^2$).
- **The Dimensionless Ratio (Primary Output):** When room dimensions are unknown, we output the dimensionless ratio:
  $$\text{ratio} = \frac{RT60_\text{measured}}{RT60_\text{target}}$$
  A ratio of $3.0$ indicates that the space requires $3\times$ its current absorption to reach the target, requiring zero knowledge of room volume.
- **Eyring Formula (Cross-Check):** When boundaries are heavily treated ($\bar{\alpha} > 0.2\text{–}0.3$, such as vocal booths and recording control rooms), Sabine underestimates the required treatment. Eyring accounts for discrete reflection loss:
  $$RT60 = \frac{0.161 \cdot V}{-S \ln(1 - \bar{\alpha})}$$

## 2. Mathematical Foundations & Implementation Specifications

### 2.1 Cooley–Tukey In-Place Butterfly (Interleaved Buffer)
In [`src/dsp/fft.js`](file:///home/equator/playground/college/LSSP%20Project/src/dsp/fft.js), complex signals are stored in an interleaved `Float64Array`:
$$\text{buf} = [\text{re}_0, \text{im}_0, \text{re}_1, \text{im}_1, \dots, \text{re}_{N-1}, \text{im}_{N-1}]$$
Complex element $k$ is stored at indices $2k$ (real) and $2k+1$ (imaginary).

1. **Bit-Reversal Permutation:** Indices $i \in [0, N-1]$ are swapped with their bit-reversed counterparts $j = \text{bitReverse}(i)$.
2. **DIT Butterfly:** For stages $\text{len} = 2, 4, \dots, N$ with twiddle angle $\theta = \pm \frac{2\pi}{\text{len}}$:
   $$W = \cos(\theta) + j \sin(\theta)$$
   $$u = x[i + j], \quad v' = W \cdot x[i + j + \text{half}]$$
   $$x[i + j] = u + v', \quad x[i + j + \text{half}] = u - v'$$
   For forward FFT: $\text{sign} = -1$. For IFFT: $\text{sign} = +1$, followed by $1/N$ normalization.

### 2.2 Exponential Sweep & Inverse Filter
Given $f_1 = 50\text{ Hz}$, $f_2 = 8000\text{ Hz}$, $T = 5\text{ s}$, $f_s = 48000\text{ Hz}$:
$$L = \frac{T}{\ln(f_2 / f_1)}, \quad K = 2\pi f_1 L$$
$$\phi(t) = K \left(e^{t / L} - 1\right), \quad x(t) = \sin(\phi(t))$$
Fade-in and fade-out use $20\text{ ms}$ half-Hann windows:
$$w_\text{in}[n] = 0.5 \left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{\pi n}{N_\text{fade}}\right)\right), \quad w_\text{out}[n] = 0.5 \left(1 + \cos\left(\frac{\pi n}{N_\text{fade}}\right)\right)$$
The Farina inverse filter is:
$$x_\text{inv}(t) = x(T - t) \cdot e^{-t / L}$$

### 2.3 Linear Convolution & Frequency-Domain Deconvolution
To avoid circular convolution time-aliasing, zero-padding must satisfy:
$$N_\text{fft} = 2^{\lceil \log_2(N_y + N_\text{inv} - 1) \rceil}$$
$$h_\text{raw} = \text{IFFT}(\text{FFT}(y, N_\text{fft}) \cdot \text{FFT}(x_\text{inv}, N_\text{fft}))$$
The direct linear arrival origin $t=0$ is at sample index $\text{len}(x) - 1$.

### 2.4 Butterworth Bilinear Transform & SOS Filtering
Analogue frequencies are pre-warped to compensate for non-linear frequency warping of the bilinear transform:
$$\Omega = 2 f_s \tan\left(\frac{\pi f}{f_s}\right)$$
For nominal centre frequency $f_c$, band edges are:
$$f_\text{lo} = \frac{f_c}{\sqrt{2}}, \quad f_\text{hi} = f_c \sqrt{2}$$
The prototype $N=2$ lowpass poles $p_k = \exp\left(j\frac{\pi(2k + 3)}{4}\right)$ transform to bandpass poles via:
$$s^2 - p \cdot BW \cdot s + \Omega_0^2 = 0 \quad (\Omega_0 = \sqrt{\Omega_\text{lo}\Omega_\text{hi}}, \; BW = \Omega_\text{hi} - \Omega_\text{lo})$$
Analogue poles map to digital poles via the Bilinear Transform:
$$z = \frac{1 + s / (2f_s)}{1 - s / (2f_s)}$$
The filter is realized as 2 Second-Order Sections (SOS biquads) evaluated causal-forward using Direct-Form II Transposed structure for numerical stability:
$$y[n] = b_0 x[n] + z_0[n-1]$$
$$z_0[n] = b_1 x[n] - a_1 y[n] + z_1[n-1]$$
$$z_1[n] = b_2 x[n] - a_2 y[n]$$

### 2.5 The Numbered 8-Step Noise-Floor Truncation & RT60 Analysis Algorithm
Implemented in [`src/dsp/rt60.js`](file:///home/equator/playground/college/LSSP%20Project/src/dsp/rt60.js):

1. **Direct Sound Alignment & Peak Power:**
   - Locate direct sound peak: $n_\text{peak} = \arg\max_n |h[n]|$.
   - Extract decay sequence: $h_\text{decay}[n] = h[n + n_\text{peak}]$.
   - Compute peak power: $P_\text{peak} = \max_n (h_\text{decay}[n]^2)$.
2. **Noise Floor Estimation ($\sigma^2$):**
   - If pre-sweep silence samples $s$ ($M > 0$) exist: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1} s[m]^2$.
   - Otherwise, estimate from the late $15\%$ tail: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{N_\text{tail}} \sum_{k=N-N_\text{tail}}^{N-1} h_\text{decay}[k]^2$.
   - $\text{DR}_\text{peak} = -10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\max(\sigma^2, 10^{-15})}{\max(P_\text{peak}, 10^{-15})}\right)$.
3. **Smoothed Squared Envelope (20 ms Window):**
   - Window length: $W = \max(\lfloor 0.02 \cdot f_s \rfloor, 1)$.
   - Centered moving average using prefix sums:
     $$\text{env}[n] = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{j=-\lfloor W/2 \rfloor}^{\lfloor (W-1)/2 \rfloor} h_\text{decay}[n+j]^2$$
4. **Truncation Point Detection (+3 dB Threshold & 50 ms Floor):**
   - Find first index $n$ where $\text{env}[n] < 2.0 \cdot \sigma^2$.
   - If found, $n_\text{trunc} = n$; else $N - 1$. Enforce $n_\text{trunc} \ge \min(\lfloor 0.05 \cdot f_s \rfloor, N-1)$.
5. **Noise Energy Compensation & Backward Integration:**
   - Subtract steady noise power with zero clamping:
     $$EDC[n] = \sum_{k=n}^{n_\text{trunc}-1} \max(0.0, h_\text{decay}^2[k] - \sigma^2)$$
   - Fallback to un-subtracted integration if $EDC[0] \le 0$.
   - Convert to decibels: $EDC_\text{dB}[n] = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\max(EDC[n], 10^{-12})}{\max(EDC[0], 10^{-12})}\right)$.
6. **Usable Dynamic Range Estimation:**
   - For truncated integration: $D_\text{usable} = \min(|EDC_\text{dB}[n_\text{trunc}-1]|, \text{DR}_\text{peak})$.
   - For un-truncated integration: $D_\text{usable} = |EDC_\text{dB}[n_\text{hit}]|$ where $\text{env}[n_\text{hit}] < 2\sigma^2$.
7. **Least-Squares Regression & $R^2$ Rule:**
   - Fit $y(t) = m \cdot t + c$ over evaluation intervals:
     - **EDT:** $0\text{ dB}$ to $-10\text{ dB}$
     - **T20:** $-5\text{ dB}$ to $-25\text{ dB}$
     - **T30:** $-5\text{ dB}$ to $-35\text{ dB}$
   - Require $\ge 10$ points and slope $m < 0$. $RT = -60 / m$.
   - Require $R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2} \ge 0.95$.
8. **Validity Tier Classification & Priority Cascade:**
   - Constants: $\text{FIT\_RANGE\_T30} = 35\text{ dB}$, $\text{FIT\_RANGE\_T20} = 25\text{ dB}$, $\text{TIER\_MARGIN} = 10\text{ dB}$, $\text{MIN\_R2} = 0.95$.
   - Effective range: $D_\text{eff} = \text{round}(D_\text{usable}, 1)$.
   - Per-metric tier:
     - `"valid"`: $D_\text{eff} \ge \text{fit range} + 10\text{ dB}$ and $R^2 \ge 0.95$.
     - `"approximate"`: $D_\text{eff} \ge \text{fit range}$ and $R^2 \ge 0.95$, but $< 10\text{ dB}$ margin.
     - `"invalid"`: fails fit range or $R^2 < 0.95$.
   - Priority selection:
     1. T30 if `valid`
     2. T20 if `valid` (valid T20 with 10 dB margin strictly preferred over approximate T30)
     3. T30 if `approximate`
     4. T20 if `approximate`
     5. Else `used: "none"`, `tier: "invalid"`, `rt60: NaN`.

### 2.6 Sabine & Eyring Treatment Formulas
Implemented in [`src/dsp/sabine.js`](file:///home/equator/playground/college/LSSP%20Project/src/dsp/sabine.js):

- **Sabine Total Absorption:**
  $$A = \frac{0.161 \cdot V}{RT60}$$
  $$\Delta A = A_\text{target} - A_\text{now} = 0.161 \cdot V \left(\frac{1}{RT60_\text{target}} - \frac{1}{RT60_\text{measured}}\right)$$
- **Treatment Panel Area:**
  Covering an existing boundary of absorption $\alpha_\text{existing}$ with acoustic material $\alpha_\text{material}$:
  $$S_\text{panel} = \frac{\Delta A}{\alpha_\text{material} - \alpha_\text{existing}}$$
- **Eyring Average Absorption & Equivalent Area:**
  $$\bar{\alpha} = 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{0.161 \cdot V}{S_\text{total} \cdot RT60}\right)$$
  $$A_\text{Eyring} = S_\text{total} \cdot \bar{\alpha}$$
  $$S_\text{panel, Eyring} = \frac{A_\text{Eyring, target} - A_\text{Eyring, now}}{\alpha_\text{material} - \alpha_\text{existing}}$$

## 5. Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Circular Convolution Aliasing in Deconvolution:**
   Computing `ifft(complexMultiply(fft(y), fft(inv)))` using an FFT size equal to $\text{len}(y)$ causes circular convolution wrap-around, contaminating early reflections with the decaying tail. The FFT buffer size must always be padded to $N_\text{fft} \ge N_y + N_\text{inv} - 1$.
2. **Integrating Before the Direct Sound Peak:**
   If Schroeder integration begins at sample $n=0$ of a recording containing pre-sweep silence or time-of-flight latency, background noise before the direct arrival is accumulated into the total energy $EDC[0]$, corrupting early decay metrics like EDT. Always align integration to the direct sound peak: $n_\text{peak} = \arg\max |h[n]|$.
3. **Floating Point Round-Off Drift in Moving Averages:**
   Computing a centered sliding window via `sum += add - drop` across $120{,}000$ float64 numbers accumulates floating-point drift over time. Using an exact prefix sum array (`pref[hi + 1] - pref[lo]`) guarantees zero numerical drift and runs in $\mathcal{O}(N)$ time.
4. **Dividing by 20 or 30 in RT60 Fit Scaling:**
   T20 measures decay over a $20\text{ dB}$ span ($-5$ to $-25\text{ dB}$); T30 measures over $30\text{ dB}$ ($-5$ to $-35\text{ dB}$). However, **both metrics estimate the time required for a full $60\text{ dB}$ decay**. The linear regression slope $m$ (in dB/s) must always be scaled by:
   $$RT = -\frac{60}{m}$$
   Dividing by 20 or 30 produces values that are $3\times$ or $2\times$ too small!
5. **Ignoring Existing Surface Absorption When Sizing Panels:**
   When adding wall panels, you cover up existing drywall or concrete ($\alpha \approx 0.05$). Sizing panels simply as $\Delta A / \alpha_\text{material}$ neglects this lost absorption, leaving the room under-treated. The correct net panel coefficient is $(\alpha_\text{material} - \alpha_\text{existing})$.
